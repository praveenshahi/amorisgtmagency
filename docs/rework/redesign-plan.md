## 1. The actual gap

Everyone can build now. That is the setup. It is not why someone hires Amoris.

The reason is this: **nobody can tell them which problem is actually the problem.**

Every specialist a founder can hire describes the problem in the shape of their own job. Sales says lead quality. Marketing says follow-up and brand. Product says roadmap. Data says instrumentation. Each is right about their slice. None of them owns the question of which slice matters — or whether the thing everyone agrees is broken is even the problem.

So the founder is left with two bad options:

1. **Decide half-blind** — pick whichever specialist argued best, and find out in two quarters.
2. **Outsource the decision** — hand it to a consultant who takes it off the desk entirely.

Founders want neither. They do not want someone else deciding for them. They also do not want to do the deep cross-domain thinking themselves — most do not have the time, and few have worked across all five domains anyway.

**What they want is the thinking done, and the decision kept.**

That is the product. Amoris is a founder-led AI GTM agency whose scarce input is a 360-degree read across **sales, marketing, product management, analytics and ML** — enough range to say the thing no specialist will: *this is not your problem, that one is.*

### The emotionally triggering line

> The most expensive thing in GTM is solving the wrong problem well.

That is the sentence that stops a founder mid-scroll. It names a fear they already have and cannot articulate: that the team is executing beautifully against a misdiagnosis, and the number still will not move.

Everything else on the page is in service of that sentence being true and earned.

### Why this converts to a paid audit fast

The audit **is** the thinking, delivered, with the decision left on the founder's desk. It is not "pay me and I'll fix it." It is: I will tell you what is actually broken, including if it is not what you think, and you decide what to do. That is why it is worth paying for immediately, and why the engagement continues afterwards — because now there is someone who can see the whole board and works alongside you rather than instead of you.

## 4. Homepage — nine sections

### 1. Hero — the misdiagnosis

- **H1 (recommended):** You're probably solving the wrong problem.
- **Alternatives:** "The most expensive thing in GTM is solving the wrong problem well." · "What if it isn't a pipeline problem?"
- **Subhead direction:** *Your sales lead says it's lead quality. Marketing says it's follow-up. Product says it's positioning. They're each right about their part, and none of them can see the whole thing. I've worked in all of it — sales, marketing, product, analytics and ML — and the most valuable thing I do is tell founders which problem is actually theirs.*
- **Category line, small:** A founder-led AI GTM agency for B2B revenue teams.
- **One CTA:** Book 30 minutes. Micro-line stating exactly what the call is.
- **Out:** hero stack chips. A tool signal in a credibility slot.

### 2. Recognition — symptoms of misdiagnosis

Plain lines. No icons, no cards, no headings-plus-body. This is the emotional core and format matters.

Direction:
- Your head of sales says it's lead quality. Marketing says it's follow-up. Both have data.
- You've fixed the thing everyone agreed was broken. Twice. The number didn't move.
- You can't tell whether last quarter was the market, the message, or the motion.
- Every specialist you hire describes the problem in the shape of their own job.
- The CRM says forty opportunities. You know six are real.
- Your best customers don't look much like the ICP on your own website.

No product, no AI, no "Amoris". Entirely about them.

### 3. Why this happens

The existing thesis copy, used **once**: outbound rarely fails at sending, it fails at deciding what to say. Extended to the diagnosis layer — the decision step takes judgment and cross-domain context, does not scale with headcount or tooling, and is invisible in activity metrics. So it is always the first casualty, and its absence shows up as a symptom somewhere else entirely.

### 4. Why nobody tells you this

- **Tools assume the diagnosis is done.** Every AI GTM tool starts after the decision — it makes sending cheaper, faster, higher-volume. Sending was never the constraint.
- **Agencies sell execution in one lane.** You hire an outbound agency and you get outbound, whether or not outbound was the problem.
- **Both are built by single-domain people.** Building an outbound tool is a few weeks of engineering now. Knowing whether outbound is even your bottleneck is not, and it does not come from a model.
- Brands are not built by funding or by shipping more. Distribution is sales and marketing working as one motion, and almost nobody has run both.

Structural argument. No named competitors, no disparagement.

### 5. Who is behind this — the conversion section

The 360 claim, made concrete and checkable.

- **Sales, marketing, product management, analytics, ML** — named as the five domains, because the range *is* the offer.
- Four years at Amazon and Meta on global data science and ML projects — pattern-finding and system design at a scale where intuition stops working.
- Then carrying a number: Great Learning (150-person org, $120K+/month net-new, LATAM and Africa), CrunchPrep (₹3.5Cr ARR, 30-person team from scratch), MsgKart (GTM from zero; Clay, LLM personalisation, n8n; first clients with one SDR, no ad spend).
- **The bridge:** the system designers have never had to explain a missed quarter. The revenue operators are waiting on an engineering team. The value is not either half — it is that one person carries both and can therefore tell which one your problem lives in.
- **The working model, explicit:** I think it through with you. You keep the decision. Founder-led means senior judgment is on your engagement directly, not delegated — there is no junior team.
- Text only. No Amazon or Meta logos. No implied endorsement. Every metric traceable to `/founder`.

### 6. How the judgment scales

Only now, the system — subordinate to the operator. Framing sentence: *the system is not the strategy; it is how good GTM decisions stop being limited by one person's hours.* Keeps Research → Reason → Judge → You decide, the HOLD explanation, all four honesty blocks (moved here from the top of the page), and the Running / In private testing split.

Reinforces the model: the system hands you a read. It never sends. The decision stays with the person whose name is on the message — the same principle as the audit, one layer down.

### 7. The stack Amoris works inside

Not a logo wall. Framing sentence, then tools grouped as text by GTM layer — Data & enrichment (Clay first), Signals & intent, Sales engagement, CRM, Revenue intelligence, Orchestration & reporting, Scheduling. Closing line: the integration layer runs on n8n and LangGraph, which makes connecting to what you already use a scoping question rather than a rebuild.

Language: "the stack I work across". Never "our integrations" or "supported platforms". No counts, no logos, no implied partnerships.

### 8. Fit and not-fit

Existing lists, adjusted for a global buyer, India-SMB-specific criteria removed. Add to not-a-fit: *anyone who wants the decision taken off their desk entirely.* Keeps the closing line about the first exclusion being the real one.

### 9. One action

One primary CTA, diagnostic framing. States call length, purpose, what the visitor leaves with, and that its job is to find out whether there is a fit at all. One secondary link maximum.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

A B2B founder landing cold can answer within thirty seconds:

1. Does this describe a problem I actually have — including one I may have misdiagnosed?
2. Who is this person, and why should I believe they can see across sales, marketing, product, analytics and ML?
3. Why is this different from the tools in my inbox and the agencies in my LinkedIn DMs?
4. Does it work with what I already run?
5. What do I do next?

Plus: thesis line appears once · no section implies a team · no logo as credential or client · one primary CTA path · correct tense on every capability claim · "agency" throughout · no price ceiling · the decision-stays-yours principle stated in sections 5, 6 and 9.
